Follow-up on the strict-IO kernel front: `tools/diag-unlock.py` on main now has **`--force-diag`** — pull the file and run:

```bash
sudo python3 tools/diag-unlock.py --bdf 0000:01:00.0 --force-diag
```

Semantics: on the first EINVAL from the BAR0 mmap it **unbinds the NVIDIA driver** (root), re-opens the mapping, runs everything (dump + `--target-offset/--value` + `--fuserows`), and **re-binds at exit** automatically. The safer route remains `iomem=relaxed` on the cmdline (persistent, no unbind); this flag is the one-boot no-reboot alternative.

Two cautions, explicit: (1) the flag is opt-in precisely because a driver unbind can hang — your box has that history (11-03, D-state); recovery = hard reset, and the unlock state survives it; (2) the re-bind at exit means the driver's init runs again at re-bind — on the refused state expect the usual RmInitAdapter failure line there, which is normal (state unchanged).

Also: your kernel-source reading + the `iomem=relaxed` note are now in our FAQ (Linux section) — credit where due; and the diag-unlock hint on EINVAL names `CONFIG_IO_STRICT_DEVMEM` explicitly instead of a bare failure.
